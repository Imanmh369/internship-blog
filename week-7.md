# Week 7 — Robots Training  

## Sunday — Raspberry Pi Troubleshooting & Project Update  
On Sunday, I focused on troubleshooting the Raspberry Pi setup since the card reader hadn’t worked during Thursday’s attempt. My colleague and I spent time problem-solving together, and later we had an update meeting with our supervisor. We explained the technical issues we were facing and also received constructive feedback on our dominant color scatter plot.  

Taking that feedback into account, we refined the design. Instead of creating separate “Nature” and “Family” buttons, we developed a more interactive approach: when a user taps the book on the sensor, the system now automatically displays the scatter plot of its dominant colors. This made the experience more seamless and engaging.  

We also met with Mathaf to present the archive project, outlining how both my colleague and I will collaborate on it.  

---

## Monday — Robot Training Introduction  
On Monday, I attended a robot training session where we were introduced to the trainers who will guide us on how to operate and program the robots. The session began with an overview of the manuals, giving us a structured understanding of the robots’ functions and setup requirements.  

We were then asked to download specific applications that support robot usage, ensuring we had the right tools to interact with them. The trainers provided detailed explanations about the robots’ design and how they work, before moving on to practical demonstrations.  

We learned how to power on and wake up the robots, and were shown the basics of interacting with them. This hands‑on introduction gave us a clear foundation for future sessions, making the robots feel less intimidating and more approachable.  

---

##  Tuesday — Hands-On Robot Practice  
On Tuesday, we continued our robot training with a stronger focus on hands-on practice. This time, we were asked to open the robot’s box, learn how to wake the robot up, and explore different ways to interact with it.  

The trainers also introduced us to two dedicated websites that will be used to manage the robots’ personas and choreograph their dances. This gave us a glimpse into how customizable and interactive the robots can be, moving beyond basic operation into creative programming and personality design.  

It was an exciting step forward — shifting from theory to practice and opening up possibilities for how these robots can be used in engaging, dynamic ways.  

---

## Wednesday — Robots in Action  
Wednesday’s training was mostly hands-on work with the robots. We were taught how to collect logs and system information from them, which gave us a deeper understanding of how they operate and how to monitor their performance.  

After more practice interacting with the robots, we shifted to a creative task: using the robots for filming content. To prepare, we drafted a script and began working on how the robots would be part of the recording process.  

This session blended technical learning and creative application, showing how the robots can be both functional tools and engaging performers.  

---

## Thursday — Independent Training  
On Thursday, we worked with the robots without the trainers, giving us the chance to identify where we were facing difficulties on our own. The main challenge turned out to be the internet connection, which affected how smoothly the robots could operate.  

Despite that, we continued practicing and gaining more confidence in handling the robots independently. By the end of the day, we had completed the training phase.  

---

##  Reflection  
This week was an opportunity to work with something completely new, and it created an amazing experience. From troubleshooting the Raspberry Pi to exploring how robots can be operated.I gained both technical skills and creative insights.
