#  Week 5 — Experiment with Museum API  

###  Sunday — Refining the Timeline & Preparing the Presentation  
On Sunday, I focused on improving the **Collection Timeline**, making it more visually appealing and interactive. I restructured the timeline so that when users land on the page, they immediately see a display of artworks by various artists — creating a more engaging first impression.  

Later in the day, I shifted my attention to preparing for Monday’s presentation. I began outlining what to include, initially planning to cover all my activities from **Week 1 to Week 2**. This preparation helped me organize my progress and think about how best to present my work in a clear, structured way.  

---

###  Monday — Week 5 Assignment & Presentation  
On Monday morning, my supervisor assigned the **Week 5 task**. After receiving the assignment, I continued refining my presentation slides and revisited my website. I made improvements to the **demographic section** — adjusting graph colors for better visual appeal and adding total counts of artworks and artists to make the data more informative.  

By 11:00, my colleagues and I were ready for the presentation. The session went smoothly, and the refinements added clarity and impact.  

---

###  Tuesday — Exploring Museum APIs & Research Task  
On Tuesday, I began working on my **Week 5 assignment** by exploring different museum APIs provided by my supervisor. After reviewing several options, I decided to focus on the **Cleveland Museum of Art API**, as it offered a particularly rich dataset that I felt would be valuable for analysis and visualization.  

At 12:00, I had a meeting with my supervisor to present my work so far. We discussed the presentation in detail, and I received constructive feedback. During the meeting, I was also tasked with conducting research on **why robots are needed in museums** — an intriguing direction that connects technology with cultural institutions.  

---

###  Wednesday — Inspiration & Use Cases for Robots in Museums  
On Wednesday, I spent time exploring different websites to gather **inspiration for timeline visualizations**. I came across several creative examples that gave me new ideas on how to make my own timeline more engaging and visually dynamic.  

Later in the day, my colleagues and I collaborated on identifying **use cases for robots in museums**. Together, we outlined many practical scenarios where robotics could support museum operations — from assisting visitors to handling repetitive tasks. This brainstorming session highlighted the potential of technology to enhance both efficiency and visitor experience.  

Afterward, I returned to working on my **timeline visualization**, applying some of the inspiration I had gathered earlier to refine its structure and design.

###  Thursday — Advancing Timeline Visualization & Meeting with Mathaf Director  
On Thursday, I continued refining the **timeline visualization**. My first experiment involved using interactive bubbles: when a user clicked on a bubble, it displayed the artist’s basic information. While this approach was engaging, I later shifted to a **linear scale design** with dots representing each artist. Clicking on a dot revealed the artist’s information within a card, and the card also included a **“See More” button** that allowed users to explore other works by the same artist. This structure felt more intuitive and provided a smoother user experience.  

Later in the day, I had a meeting with **Zeina, the director of Mathaf**, to present the progress both my colleague and I had made. The discussion was insightful, and I received a **positive review** of my work, which was encouraging and motivating. It reinforced the value of combining technical creativity with clear presentation.
